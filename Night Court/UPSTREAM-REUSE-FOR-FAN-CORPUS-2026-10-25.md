# Borrow-first upstream adoption review for the Night Court fan corpus

**Verified 2026-10-25, public upstream entrypoints and repository state.** Intended use: private, source-grounded screenplays and fandom knowledge for all 193 episodes of the 1984–1992 original series. Not a claim that external models have been installed, licensed content downloaded, measured on our episodes, or that more original film has been reviewed. [Product contract](FAN-OBSESSION-DELIVERABLE-2026-10-25.md) and [script priorities](SCRIPT-PRIORITIES-AND-ACCEPTANCE-2026-10-25.md) control adoption decisions.

## Avoid premature reinvention and over-engineering

- **Keep our original held captions as dialogue source text.** External ASR is a discrepancy locator, not the preferred replacement for known reliable lines. This differs from generic movie-to-script projects that have no captions.
- **Solve rank 0 then rank 1**, not the other way around. IMDb/wiki quotations and character-roster data are valuable *anchors*, but their wording/source might originate from the same subtitle file. Do not count dependent quotations as independent acoustic speaker confirmation. The existing [ordered quote-aligner PR #431](https://github.com/gamesbyian/general-purpose/pull/431) addresses lexical matching only. A grounded speaker claim needs more than lexical overlap.
- **No high-cost full-episode inference first.** Existing Qwen3-VL-2B CPU runs on two-second source clips took tens to hundreds of seconds and got a touch-direction wrong. Evaluate a small set of impactful difficult actions with source-reviewed actor/action/target labels. Dense frame extraction by itself is not an accuracy strategy.
- **Model agreement isn't source verification.** Agreement can be a cheap triage score if alternatives differ architecturally; shared training data, caption leakage and a common preceding hypothesis can correlate their errors.
- **Prefer one clip call that produces multiple candidate fields** (setting, visible entities, objects, expressions, action actor/recipient, reactions, continuity/shot membership), tracked independently. Use short cut-safe moving clips over decontextualized keyframes for physical comedy.
- **Don't mistake shot cuts for story scenes.** Structure each event around shot IDs and larger setting/plot/scene episodes. Oxford's adjacent-shot context and ShotParser's registry are complementary, not replacements for scene-level understanding.
- **No 'funny, therefore true' confidence shortcuts.** Comedy explanations, fan theories and character motive readings are valuable as tagged interpretation records, and can be surfaced conversationally as interpretation. Exact quotations and concrete stage action need stronger evidence.

## Straightforward implementation sequence

1. **Schema adapter (cheap):** add normalized `episode`, `scene`, `turn`, `delivery_span`, `physical_beat`, `comedic_beat`, `character`, `continuity_link` objects. Translate borrowed outputs **into** these, rather than making a unique database per upstream project. Capture original cue refs and source SHAs, exact original text and modality-specific evidence.
2. **Retrieval seed (cheap):** derive private episode/scene chunks directly from our current 193 source scripts, substituting the richer v5 pilot manuscripts when present. Label corpus evidence tiers, retain metadata/scene cues and an unambiguous WARNING that non-pilot scaffold 'scene' boundaries are just subtitle gaps. This delivers immediate source material for LLM retrieval even before further multimedia.
3. **Pilot P0/P1 trial (highest ROI):** evaluate DramaSR's evidence aggregation on the S01 painter opening, rapid short replies, unknown adjacent cues, and S09 untimed conversational ambiguity. Measure actual boundary/identity corrections, not simply lower UNKNOWN count.
4. **Pilot P2 (medium ROI):** use line-specific sound evidence to propose mechanics first (pause, overtalk, whisper, stressed word, shouting), context-based affect separately. Compare sarcasm/exasperation/coyness on source-audited positive and negative samples.
5. **Pilot P3/P4 (selective):** compare Oxford shot-neighbor context and source-event actor/recipient checks against current Qwen descriptions. If a tested model reverses an important gesture, **do not** promote the candidate. Use supported wardrobe/props without requiring every still to be exhaustively named.
6. **Fandom synthesis:** from those same scene objects, extract coherent recurring joke and character-arc facts with precise scene/episode/source pointers. Ask follow-up retrieval questions requiring memory across episodes; evaluate abstentions and hallucinations explicitly.
7. **Scale once quality improves:** carry successful decisions across remaining held episode sources; only upscale compute, video capture or paid APIs when a paired pilot demonstrates editorial or final-fan-answer yield per minute.

## Upstream licensing, legal and provenance housekeeping

Source code: preserve MIT/Apache notices on actual incorporated files, record upstream repository + commit SHA, modifications, dependencies, model card terms and license, version pin and provenance. Dataset/movie content: research publication or an online quote page does **not** itself grant broad redistribution or model-training rights. Keep private owner media private, no original video, audio, stills, transcript bulk export, face embeddings, weights, API secrets or substantial third-party quote collections in newly public artifacts.

The *purpose is practical use*. A cloned codebase that costs hours to run, or a hundred unreviewed descriptions that misidentify the actor doing the gag, is worth less than one reliably corrected *Night Court* conversation.
