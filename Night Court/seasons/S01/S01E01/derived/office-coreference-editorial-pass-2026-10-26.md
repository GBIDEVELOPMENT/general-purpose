### Courier/judge-in-office exchange
The source reads as a brief disagreement about whether the man claiming the office is actually the judge. Lana is already known to doubt Harry's identity; Harry then says he could not perform a privileged office gesture if he were not the judge.

- “In his office?” is a likely **Lana** skeptical follow-up to Harry's physical demonstration, or another staff member challenging its propriety.
- “No, I guess not” is probably a **different respondent** conceding the point, but whether Lana replies to herself or another participant answers is ambiguous. Do not impose Lana on both utterances.
- “Here you go” occurs immediately before Harry's “Thanks. Sorry, I don't tip” to the person helping him, a strong **response/action constraint** suggesting a service/delivery participant such as the courier, rather than Harry himself.
- “Cover me” follows Harry's joke about spending a tip on a Barry Manilow album. Its addressee and performer are unclear; a pronoun-free command alone does not identify the courier, Dan or Sheila.

**Evidence quality:** medium candidate for service-role “Here you go”; low for Lana “In his office?”; UNKNOWN for the others. No new source-exact joins or splits recorded.

### Third-party courtroom dialogue and court staff
The declaration “I cannot work with that man. He's disrupting my court” must refer to Harry in third person, so Harry is excluded absent marked quotation or self-parody. Lana or Sheila are possible speakers; the court-role possessive “my court” somewhat favors court clerk Lana, but employees can refer to their working court and this is not enough to collapse the alternatives. Dan's succeeding explanation of the impossibility of removing a judge answers the complaint. “We have to kill him” logically concludes the preceding character's desperate interpretation, but its precise speaker needs the current role ledger and audio; it could be a continuation of the questioner (“So, what you're saying is...”) rather than Dan.
